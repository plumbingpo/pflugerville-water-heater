No Surprises

Honest, Upfront Pricing

No homeowner likes surprises on the final bill. That's why Pflugerville Water Heater quotes every job before the work begins, after a full diagnostic that covers the heater's age, fuel type, and capacity, the burner assembly or heating elements, the anode rod and relief valve, and the vent and gas connections. You'll know what's wrong, what the fix involves, and exactly what it will cost before you say yes.

Many water heater problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Sediment builds up on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod slowly corrodes away, and the steel underneath starts to rust from the inside with no outward warning. If you notice rusty hot water, popping or rumbling sounds, a rotten-egg smell, hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, or moisture around the base, call for an inspection right away. Catching it early often keeps a fixable issue from turning into a flooded floor.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Many homeowners ask us to size up an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with regular descaling), a replacement on your own timeline beats scrambling for a new heater with water on the floor. We'll give you an honest read on how much life your unit has left.

Preventive Care

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing year-round. Regular service prevents failures before they start, clears out sediment, and catches small faults while they're still inexpensive to fix. At Pflugerville Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your actual heater, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a burner and flue check on gas units, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a thermostat setting check.

Many homes do best with one flush a year, and homes with hard water may need two. We adjust the schedule to the age of your heater, how hard your household runs it, and the mineral content of your water, which controls how quickly sediment accumulates and how long an anode rod lasts.

The savings show up quickly. A flushed tank heats water more efficiently, a replaced anode rod can add years to the tank, and a tested relief valve is ready to do its job if pressure ever climbs too high. Next to the cost of an early replacement, regular maintenance is money well spent.

Sized for Your Household

The Right Size Water Heater for Your Home

Proper sizing matters more than most homeowners realize. Before recommending a new heater, Pflugerville Water Heater works through the details that drive your household's peak demand: how many people and bathrooms you have, showers that run back to back, when the laundry and dishwasher run, and how cold the water entering the house gets. The result is a unit with a first-hour rating or GPM capacity to cover your morning rush without paying to heat water you'll never use.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising hot water demands. A 40-gallon tank that served a couple rarely keeps up once kids are showering back to back, and a tankless water heater can deliver steady hot water as long as it's sized for the flow. If you're weighing tank against tankless, or gas against a heat pump model, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: installed price, operating cost, gas line and venting requirements, electrical capacity, and the footprint, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Frequent Failures

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

The majority of service calls we get come down to a handful of recurring problems. There's no hot water at all. The gas burner won't light, or it lights and shuts off. The hot water runs out fast, the tank pops every time the burner fires, or the tankless unit locks out with an error code. Rusty water, a rotten-egg smell, and a dripping relief valve round out the list. We fix every one of them, starting with the exact part that failed.

Some problems are just a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A burned-out element simply leaves you with lukewarm water, but a relief valve that drips or discharges is a warning that pressure or temperature in the tank is running too high, and a thermostat set above 120°F can burn a child in seconds. A gas smell near the heater means leave the house first and call the gas utility from outside, then call us. We match the fix to the failure: elements and thermostats on electric tanks, thermocouples, flame sensors, and gas valves on gas units, expansion tanks where pressure is spiking, and leak repair on drain valves, fittings, and relief lines.
