Warning Signs to Watch For

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • A second or third repair within a year or two
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old
  • A slow leak or damp spot under the tank itself
  • Discolored hot water, a sign the tank is corroding inside
  • Rust or corrosion on the tank, fittings, or vent connector
  • Less and less hot water, even after repairs
  • Energy bills rising while hot water gets harder to come by

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here's where to begin:

  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
  • Have water pressure checked; above about 80 psi, a pressure reducing valve protects the new heater
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Descale a tankless unit every year, or more often with hard water
  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
