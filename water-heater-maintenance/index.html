If you could see inside your water heater, the case for maintenance would make itself. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, causes that familiar rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, built to corrode in place of the tank, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. For homeowners in Pflugerville, TX, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

A Pflugerville Water Heater visit is designed to stop both of those processes early. We drain and flush the tank until the water runs clear, pull and inspect the anode rod and recommend a replacement when it is mostly consumed, and test the temperature and pressure relief valve to confirm it opens and reseats. On gas units we inspect the burner, pilot or igniter, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue baffle, and venting for proper draft. On electric units we test the elements and thermostats with a meter and look at the wiring for heat damage. We check the expansion tank's air charge against your water pressure, confirm the thermostat setting is near 120 degrees to limit scalding, and look for early leaks at fittings and valves.

Tankless water heaters have their own maintenance needs. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers error codes, reduced flow, or temperature swings. We descale the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. At the end of any visit, you get a plain-language report of what we found. Call (737) 422-3568 to schedule water heater maintenance in Pflugerville before a small issue turns into a leak.

Signs You Need Water Heater Maintenance in Pflugerville

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Rumbling, popping, or crackling from the tank as it heats
  • Hot water with a sulfur odor
  • Temperature swings or reduced flow from a tankless heater
  • Rising utility bills that point to lost efficiency
  • Hot water that looks cloudy or rust-colored
  • Less hot water available than when the tank was new
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • Moisture, drips, or rust around the base or fittings

Why Professional Water Heater Maintenance Matters

Some upkeep is fair game for homeowners: watching for leaks, keeping the area around the heater clear, and noting new noises. Beyond that, the work gets riskier. Draining a tank means handling scalding water and a drain valve that may not close again if it is old and full of sediment. Pulling an anode rod often takes a breaker bar and real leverage on a fitting that has not moved in years. Testing a T&P valve on an older heater can leave it dripping afterward. On gas units, the burner, thermocouple, and venting involve combustion safety, and a vent that is not drafting can put carbon monoxide into the house.

We come prepared for what an older heater throws at us: replacement drain valves, anode rods sized for your tank, flexible anodes for tight clearances, T&P valves, and meters for testing elements and thermostats. For tankless systems, we bring the pump and descaling setup. Just as important, we know what to look for, from early corrosion at the fittings to a thermocouple starting to weaken, so you can plan a repair before it becomes a failure. The goal is simple: a heater that lasts longer, runs more efficiently, and does not surprise you with a leak.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Schedule a flush every year, or every six months if you have hard water
  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Stay on top of tankless descaling to avoid error codes and reduced flow
  • Consider a water softener if hard water keeps building sediment and scale
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
  • Replace a heavily consumed anode rod to extend the tank's life
  • Leave open space around the tank, especially near a gas burner
