Warning Signs to Watch For

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Trouble reaching sanitation temperatures in the kitchen
  • Higher utility bills that point to a heater losing efficiency
  • Fixtures that run too hot one minute and cool the next
  • An aging unit you would rather replace on your schedule than in an emergency
  • Long waits for hot water at sinks far from the heater
  • Visible corrosion or water pooling near the tank
  • A commercial heater that has grown noticeably louder
  • The tank giving up in the middle of a rush

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here's where to begin:

  • Keep recirculation pumps and check valves in service to avoid long waits and wasted energy
  • Schedule upgrades during slow periods instead of waiting for a breakdown
  • Record fixture temperatures regularly, especially before inspections
  • Leave space around commercial heaters for airflow and safe servicing
  • Replace consumed anode rods before the tank itself starts to corrode
  • Flush commercial tanks more often than residential ones, often every few months in heavy use or hard water
  • Have mixing valves checked so fixtures stay neither scalding nor lukewarm
