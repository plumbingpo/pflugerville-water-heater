How Round Rock Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Most premature tank failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard so the heat exchanger stays clean.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular service, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or a damp spot under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a flooded utility room.

Common Water Heater Problems in Round Rock

Whatever type of water heater your Round Rock home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tank heaters tend to have trouble with thermocouples and flame sensors, dirty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric tanks lose heating elements and thermostats, especially when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless heaters show error codes for scale buildup, ignition faults, and flow sensor problems, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. Our technicians work on all four every week and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
